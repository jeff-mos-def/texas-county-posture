## What to watch

- **Water.** Dripping Springs, Driftwood and Wimberley draw on the Trinity Aquifer. In 2025, Charlie Flatten, general manager of the Hays Trinity Groundwater Conservation District, projected that the district's groundwater allocations will reach the total supply available under Texas Water Development Board modeling by 2036. In the east, the Barton Springs/Edwards Aquifer Conservation District supplies groundwater to nearly 100,000 people in south Austin, southern Travis County and eastern Hays County, including Buda and Kyle. As of August 2026 it had been in Stage 3 Exceptional Drought since October 2025, only the second time in its 38-year history.
- **The last Republican seats.** From January, Precinct 3 Commissioner Morgan Hammer will be the only Republican on the Commissioners Court. She won the seat in 2024 with 52.79% over Democrat Rebecca Minnick, and her term expires in 2028. Sheriff Anthony Hipolito Jr., a Republican, won in 2024 with 52.67% while Kamala Harris carried the county. Both offices are on the 2028 ballot.
- **2028 turnout.** The 2026 Senate margin came with [[turnout.2026.ballots|count]] ballots cast, compared with 126,683 in 2024, when Harris carried the county by [[marginHistory.2024.margin|margin]]. The 2028 presidential race will show how much of the gap between those two results holds.

Hays's Democratic margin in the Senate race was nearly three times its 2022 margin for governor, and Democrats down the ballot won by less.

## Down-ballot

<DownBallot />

Democrat Aidee Hernandez unseated Republican County Judge Connie Scott by [[downBallot.county-judge.margin|margin]], a difference of 365 votes, running [[derived.downBallot.county-judge.vsTop|points]] points behind the Senate result. Leanna Gutierrez Gonzales unseated County Clerk Kara Sands by [[downBallot.county-clerk.margin|margin]], and Maria Carolina (Kina) Ventura unseated District Clerk Anne Lorentzen by [[downBallot.district-clerk.margin|margin]]. Sands and Lorentzen, both Republicans, had held their offices since 2015. Democratic judges Lisa Gonzales and Deeanne Svoboda Galvan held County Courts-at-Law No. 2 and No. 3 by [[downBallot.county-court-at-law-2.margin|margin]] and [[downBallot.county-court-at-law-3.margin|margin]].

Democrat Isabel Noyola-Martin won Commissioner Precinct 2 with 63.33% after beating Commissioner Joe A. Gonzalez in the Democratic primary runoff, 54.09% to 45.91%. Democrat Stephanie Guerrero Saenz unseated Republican Rep. Denise Villalobos in House District 34, 55.58% to 44.42% in the county's official count.

## What to watch

- **Water.** Combined storage in Lake Corpus Christi and Choke Canyon Reservoir, the City of Corpus Christi's western supply, fell to a record low of 7.7% in May 2026. The city spent 596 days under Stage 3 restrictions before July rains let it ease to Stage 2 on Aug. 4, 2026, and then to Stage 1. Combined storage stood at 37.1% on Nov. 6, and the city's projected water emergency date has moved to September 2028.
- **Commissioners Court.** Republicans held three of the five seats before the election. From January, Democrats will hold three: Hernandez, Noyola-Martin in Precinct 2 and John Marez in Precinct 3. Marez won Precinct 3 in 2024 with 52.21%, and Republican Mike Pusley won Precinct 1 with 59.41%. Both seats are on the 2028 ballot, along with Sheriff J.C. Hooper and County Attorney Jenny Dorsey, Republicans who were unopposed in 2024.
- **2028 turnout.** The 2026 Senate margin came with [[turnout.2026.ballots|count]] ballots cast, compared with 122,339 in 2024, when Donald Trump carried the county by [[marginHistory.2024.margin|margin]]. The 2028 presidential race will show how much of that reversal holds.

Nueces moved from a Republican margin in the 2022 governor's race to a Democratic margin in the 2026 Senate race, and Democrats won every contested countywide office, each by less than the Senate margin.
